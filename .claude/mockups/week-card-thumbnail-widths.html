What the extra width actually buys

Widening the columns does make the preview bigger — but stacked above the text it makes the column taller at the same time, so you trade a vertical scroll for a lateral one and still get both. The third scenario spends the width differently, and that one is worth a look.

That last number is the surprise and it is not a rounding artefact: at 240px the titles wrap to fewer lines, and the height saved doing that is slightly more than the preview costs. You get a real picture on every post and a shorter column than you have now — the bill is paid in lateral scroll instead.

Each strip below is a 1120px board viewport — a 1440px screen minus your sidebar and page padding, which is exactly where today's 153px column comes from. Where the board is wider than that, it is clipped here the way it would be on screen. Real Monday, real art, real crop rule.

Where I land

Scenario 3. Stacking a portrait preview above the text is what makes the column explode — the image is doing all the work vertically. Put it beside the text and the same photo, at the same true aspect, costs a fraction of the height while staying perfectly readable.

It also explains why the 40px tile felt useless earlier: it was not the side-by-side layout that was wrong, it was that 153px is too narrow to give the image a workable share of it. At 240px there is room for both.

The honest cost of any lateral scroll: Saturday and Sunday leave the screen. "See my whole week" is arguably the point of this view, so if that trade is wrong, say so and I will build scenario 1 — it is the only one that keeps all seven days visible.
